<objective>
Make source-chip health detail reachable at mobile-width viewports by guaranteeing the chip row always seats at least one chip, so the health popover always has a trigger.

Purpose: closes gap **G-14-2** from `14-UAT.md` Test 2 — "the popover is not shown at all unless the viewport is wide enough to accommodate it". The debug session (`.planning/debug/popover-hidden-narrow-viewport.md`) proved this is NOT a tooltip-rendering defect: the styled popover wraps at 320px, flips, shifts and stays fully visible down to 375px in both Chromium and Firefox **whenever a trigger exists**. The failure is trigger disappearance — `visibleChipCount` floors at zero, so at 375px a long-named chip (the button caps at `max-w-48`, whole chip about 230px) is relegated wholesale into the "+N" pill and the row contains no chip at all. Pre-existing Phase 6 overflow behaviour; phase 14's UAT was simply the first narrow-viewport visual check of this surface.

Output: a budget-gated floor of one inline chip, a chip that truncates harder rather than clipping the row's reserved controls, a Playwright spec pinning "chip health detail reachable at 375px", and the pre-existing narrow-width specs brought onto the new guarantee.

Gap source: `14-UAT.md` § Gaps (G-14-2, severity major). Diagnosis: `.planning/debug/popover-hidden-narrow-viewport.md`.
</objective>

<fix_direction_decision>
The diagnosis surfaced three candidate directions. This plan takes the first, and records why the other two are not taken — the executor implements this decision, it is not re-opened at execution time.

**Chosen — guarantee at least one chip stays in the row, truncated harder.**
It restores the ORIGINAL health surface (the chip's own popover plus its `aria-describedby` description) rather than inventing a second one, so it introduces zero new copy, zero new component, zero new palette/spacing/type value, and needs no change to `14-UI-SPEC.md`'s "Explicit Reuse — Zero New UI" table. It also restores something the current behaviour loses entirely: a per-source health DOT at narrow width. Today at 375px that source contributes only to the aggregated tone dot on the "+N" pill; after the fix its own dot is back on screen. It is confined to one pure function, one call site, and two CSS class decisions.

**Not taken — make the "+N" pill carry the relegated chips' health surface.**
The pill already carries `worstHealthTone(hiddenSources)` as a visible dot, so the "something in here is unhealthy" signal exists. Giving it a *sentence* means either composing new summary copy that `14-UI-SPEC.md` does not specify (and this phase's UI contract is explicitly "zero new copy beyond G2–G4"), or lifting `SourceChip.svelte`'s `tooltipText` derivation into `format.ts` so two surfaces can share it — a refactor that would invalidate the structural assertions in `source-chip-tooltip.test.ts`, which read the component source directly. It would also require nesting a `Tooltip` around an existing `PopoverTrigger`, composing two floating primitives and their competing `aria-describedby`/`aria-expanded` wiring on one element. Too much surface for a gap closure. The path it would improve — open "+N", hover a clone — already works correctly, verified cross-engine in the debug session.

**Not taken — a row-independent narrow-viewport health surface.**
A new surface contradicts the phase's own UI contract and is the largest of the three changes for the smallest marginal reachability gain over the chosen direction.

**Also considered and rejected — narrowing the chip's width cap below 768px.**
A `max-md:` cap on the display-name button lowers the relegation threshold but does not *guarantee* anything: with more instances or wider reserved controls the row can still end up empty. It would also truncate names harder at every narrow width, including widths with room to spare. The budget-gated floor gives a guarantee and only ever engages where the alternative was an empty row.
</fix_direction_decision>
