<objective>
Make the header usable with a thumb. Below 768px the chip actions trigger stops hiding behind a
hover state that a touchscreen can never produce, chip health detail becomes reachable by long
press, and the purely decorative branding lockup steps aside to give the webspace switcher room.
At 768px and above, the header behaves exactly as it does today.

Purpose: CONTEXT.md offers the narrow-width header to Claude's discretion under one hard
constraint — "every existing affordance must remain reachable on touch (chip menu, filter
toggle, refresh, manage-sources) even if relocated; nothing may silently disappear." Two of
those affordances are gated on hover today. RESEARCH Pitfall 2 is explicit that
`source-chip-pill.test.ts`'s hover-only reveal assertions describe exactly the affordance that
needs a touch equivalent, and that the guard must gain a deliberate new assertion rather than
coincidentally still passing.

Output: a touch-reachable chip menu and health detail, a hidden-below-768px branding lockup, a
widened chip guard, and a mobile e2e spec that taps rather than hovers.

<planner_resolutions>
Three discretion calls this plan LOCKS (CONTEXT.md offers the whole narrow-width header area;
these are the choices, recorded so an executor does not re-open them).

**R1 — The chip actions trigger becomes always-visible below 768px, rather than gaining a new
reveal gesture.**
The existing reveal is `opacity-0` lifted by `group-hover` or `group-has-[:focus-visible]`.
Neither event exists on a touchscreen, so the control is unreachable there today. Adding a
`max-md:opacity-100` clause is the minimal correct fix: the reveal machinery stays intact and
untouched at 768px and above (preserving Phase 6's G-06-3b resolution exactly), and below it the
trigger simply has nothing left to reveal from. Inventing a tap-to-reveal gesture would add a
second interaction model for one control and would conflict with the chip's primary tap action,
which is the filter toggle.

**R2 — Chip health detail degrades to a native `title`, not a new touch-activated tooltip.**
`bits-ui`'s `Tooltip` has no built-in touch-activation mode, and building one is a component-
library project, not a release-UX fix. A native `title` attribute is long-press-accessible on
most mobile browsers, costs nothing at desktop width (the richer `Tooltip` fires first there),
and — critically — is bound from the SAME `tooltipText` derived value the `Tooltip` renders, so
the two copies cannot drift. The chip's coloured health dot remains the always-visible primary
signal at every width regardless of whether the detailed text is reachable, which is D-07's
health-honesty framing applied to the header rather than to the row.

**R3 — The chip `⋮` trigger keeps its documented 32px sub-floor exception.**
06-UI-SPEC.md G-06-3b records a deliberate `size-8` exception to the 44px touch floor, on the
grounds that a 44px control cannot fit padding-free inside a 44px-tall pill. That geometric
constraint is width-independent and still holds. 09.1-UI-SPEC.md carries the exception forward
while recording honestly that its original "desktop-only, pointer precision" rationale no longer
strictly applies now that this viewport is real mobile traffic. Changing it would mean either
shrinking the pill below the 44px floor or growing it past 44px only at narrow widths —
fragmenting the chip's established geometry across breakpoints for a control this phase does not
otherwise need to touch. Do not add `size-11` to that trigger: `source-chip-pill.test.ts`
explicitly asserts its absence, because a 44px child re-inflates the pill and reopens G-06-3b's
first defect.
</planner_resolutions>
